# Unit Commitment Problem

## **Overview**

The Unit Commitment (UC) problem is a fundamental optimisation challenge in power system operations. It involves determining which generators should be turned on or off over a given time horizon and how much power each generator should produce to meet electricity demand at minimum cost. The model also considers operational constraints such as generator limits, ramping capabilities, and start-up/shutdown costs.

---

## **Problem Description**

The goal is to schedule the operation of multiple generators to:

- Meet the electricity demand for all loads at each time period.
- Minimise the total cost, which includes:
  - Generation (dispatch) costs.
  - Start-up and shutdown costs.
  - Penalties for load shedding (unserved demand).

This is done while respecting technical constraints such as generation limits, ramp rates, minimum downtime, and maximum start-ups.

---

## **Inputs**

- `time_horizon`: Number of time periods in the planning horizon.
- `num_of_generators`: Total number of generators.
- `num_of_loads`: Total number of loads.
- `gen_max[g,t]`: Maximum generation capacity of generator `g` at time `t`.
- `gen_min[g,t]`: Minimum generation level if generator `g` is on at time `t`.
- `init_commitment[g]`: Initial on/off status of generator `g`.
- `demand[l,t]`: Load demand for load `l` at time `t`.
- `dispatch_cost[g,t]`: Cost per unit of power generated by generator `g` at time `t`.
- `startup_cost[g]`: Cost to start generator `g`.
- `shutdown_cost[g]`: Cost to shut down generator `g`.
- `shed_cost[l]`: Penalty cost for not serving load `l`.
- `max_ramp_rate[g]`: Maximum change in generation for generator `g` between consecutive periods.
- `min_down[g]`: Minimum downtime after a generator shuts down.
- `max_num_start[g]`: Maximum number of start-ups allowed for generator `g`.

---

## **Decision Variables**

- `commitment[g,t]`: Boolean indicating if generator `g` is on at time `t`.
- `generation[g,t]`: Power output of generator `g` at time `t`.
- `loss_of_load[l,t]`: Amount of demand not served for load `l` at time `t`.
- `up[g,t]`: Boolean indicating if generator `g` starts up at time `t`.
- `down[g,t]`: Boolean indicating if generator `g` shuts down at time `t`.

---

## **Constraints**

1. **Generation Limits:**
   - If a generator is on, its output must be between `gen_min` and `gen_max`.
2. **Load Balance:**
   - Total generation equals total demand minus any loss of load.
3. **Start-up and Shutdown Logic:**
   - `up[g,t]` and `down[g,t]` track changes in commitment status.
4. **Ramping Constraints:**
   - Limits on how quickly generation can change between periods.
5. **Minimum Downtime:**
   - After shutting down, a generator must remain off for a minimum number of periods.
6. **Start-up Limit:**
   - Each generator can only start up a limited number of times.
7. **Loss of Load:**
   - Cannot exceed the demand for any load.

---

## **Objective**

Minimise the total operational cost:
\[
\text{Total Cost} = \sum*{g,t} (\text{dispatch_cost} \times \text{generation}) + \sum*{g,t} (\text{startup_cost} \times \text{up}) + \sum*{g,t} (\text{shutdown_cost} \times \text{down}) + \sum*{l,t} (\text{shed_cost} \times \text{loss_of_load})
\]

- Power system scheduling and planning.
- Electricity market operations.
- Integration of renewable energy sources.
